Part 3.2: Results and Comparisons
Each working image is capped at 1000 pixels on its longest side before comparison, following the assignment's medium-size image guidance.
The target width is the working width minus round(0.30 * working width); the PDF says about 30% and gives no further rounding rule. All three narrowed methods preserve height and use that same target width.
Normal resizing uses OpenCV INTER_AREA. Center cropping removes equal horizontal margins. Seam carving uses Part 1.2 finite-difference magnitude for the six standard comparisons.

Observed failure case: A failure case.jpg, a tightly framed five-person group photo. The seam overlay shows paths passing through human silhouettes rather than removing only background; the carved result narrows the gaps and locally compresses bodies, although all five people remain recognizable. Within the central people region (x=5%-95%, y=15%-85%), 85471 of 93654 seam-row positions (91.3%) pass through the group. Gradient energy measures local intensity change, not semantic importance; low-contrast clothing and spaces between bodies can remain inexpensive paths, so repeated seam removal damages the group.
Observed comparison: inspect each four-panel figure for structures that remain recognizable under seam carving versus details removed by center crop. The person-outdoors figure preserves the person's height and proportions better than normal resizing.
The finite-difference and DoG energy maps differ in smoothing and edge response; their distinct seam sets are shown in the energy-comparison figure, with finite-difference paths in red and DoG paths in cyan.
